# Architecture, Not Jurisdiction, Decides Moneta's Licences

Moneta's quickest lawful launch is to US residents under the licences of Dakota Platform, Inc. That route opens only after two changes: the app must stop being the only holder of the signing key over user wallets, and it must stop recording user-to-user transfers in its own database. **No offshore, Swiss or Canadian licence removes that step.** As built, FinCEN's 2019 guidance and the "control" test now written into most state money-transmission laws make **Moneta itself a money transmitter**, which Dakota's licences cannot cover. A user key that must sign every send, plus transfers settled on-chain through Dakota, would keep Moneta a software front end. Two problems in the contracts sit on top of this. First, **Dakota's public Terms and its Lead Bank funds-transfer agreement are written for commercial use only and disclaim Regulation E**. Second, Dakota lists licences in **20 jurisdictions, and California and New York are not among them**. A key-holding app cannot enter either state without its own DFAL licence or BitLicense. Getting Moneta's own US licences means registering with FinCEN and filing state by state through NMLS. That costs about **$65,000 in application fees for the 32 states in the multistate programme alone**, plus bonds totalling millions, and reviews take from weeks to two years. Launching first, as built, would count as "prior unlicensed activity", which bars Moneta from the multistate programme. **No offshore entity may lawfully serve US residents.** A credible offshore *bank* needs **US$10 million or more** of capital, so the realistic offshore option is a digital-asset licence for a separate base of non-US customers, in Bermuda, Cayman, Abu Dhabi or similar, from about US$100,000. Switzerland's new payment and crypto-institution licences are still a consultation draft. The EU requires both a MiCA authorisation and an e-money licence for USDC accounts. Incorporating in Canada, or having staff there, can make Moneta a FINTRAC money services business even if every customer is American. Three things come before any filing: forming the entity, a written memo from counsel on Moneta's regulatory status, and Dakota's written answers on custody, consumer use and state coverage. This is research current to 24 November 2026. It is not legal advice.

The customer choice changes the difficulty more than the jurisdiction does. **A business-first launch fits Dakota's contracts as they stand.** Dakota's Terms already limit use to commercial purposes, and Dakota onboards non-US businesses: it requires an EIN only from US firms and claims coverage of more than 140 countries ([Dakota business onboarding](https://support-platform.dakota.xyz/articles/2551929093-onboarding-your-business-account-customers); [Dakota](https://dakota.xyz/)). The custody redesign is still needed, and each business's home country may impose its own cross-border rules. **US consumers are the hardest segment under the contracts**, because they bring in Regulation E, the restrictions on the word "bank" and the California–New York gap. Non-US consumers need a separate entity licensed where they live. One reason is that Visa licenses each card BIN "for use in a single country" ([Visa Rules](https://bo.review.visa.com/content/dam/VCOM/download/visa-rules-public.pdf)). The other is that Dakota bars residents of 35 jurisdictions, and transactions with them, including China, Kenya, Pakistan and Ukraine ([Dakota prohibited jurisdictions](https://support-platform.dakota.xyz/articles/7395041356-prohibited-jurisdictions)).

## One signing key and a private ledger make Moneta a transmitter everywhere

Dakota tells clients that "your flows run under Dakota's licenses, and regulators examine Dakota directly" ([Dakota Trust](https://www.dakota.xyz/trust)). But a licence covers only what the licensee itself does. FinCEN defines money transmission as accepting value from one person and transmitting it to another "by any means" ([31 CFR 1010.100(ff)(5)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-A/section-1010.100)). Its 2019 virtual-currency guidance calls hosted-wallet providers "account-based money transmitters". It names their hallmark as **"total independent control over the value (although it is contractually obligated to access the value only on instructions from the owner)"**. The guidance adds that a provider whose customers' value is "represented as an entry in the accounts of the provider" is a transmitter "regardless of the label the person applies to itself or its activities" ([FinCEN FIN-2019-G001](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20CVC%20Guidance%20FINAL.pdf)).

Moneta fits that description twice. Its server is the only signer, with an approval threshold of one, so it can move any user's USDC alone. Its user-to-user transfers are entries in its own database that Dakota never sees, so the acceptance and transmission are Moneta's, not Dakota's. None of the recognised exits fits. The agent exemption covers only a business that is an MSB "solely" because it acts as another MSB's agent ([31 CFR 1022.380](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1022/subpart-D/section-1022.380)). Dakota's public Terms make Dakota the *customer's* agent and never appoint a platform as Dakota's agent ([Dakota Terms](https://www.dakota.xyz/terms)). The payment-processor exemption requires a purchase of goods or services through a clearing system open only to BSA-regulated institutions ([FIN-2019-G001](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20CVC%20Guidance%20FINAL.pdf)). A peer infrastructure provider's contract shows how the industry reads these facts. Bridge's developer agreement forbids platforms to **"hold or control any private cryptographic keys that provide access to crypto-assets or funds held on behalf of Users"** ([Bridge Developer Agreement](https://bridge.xyz/legal/developer-agreement)).

State law and every foreign regime studied reach the same answer. The Money Transmission Modernization Act (MTMA) defines control of virtual currency as "the power to execute unilaterally or prevent indefinitely a virtual currency transaction" ([CSBS MTMA](https://www.csbs.org/sites/default/files/2023-02/CSBS%20Money%20Transmission%20Modernization%20Act.pdf)). About 35 states had enacted it in some form by autumn 2026, although counts differ ([Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/insights/102o0z1/csbs-launches-and-formalizes-money-transmission-modernization-act-guidance-reques)). Washington exempts wallet storage only where the holder "does not have the ability to use that virtual currency without the consent or permission of the actual owner" ([WA DFI](https://dfi.wa.gov/cs-newsletter/2018/issue3/virtual-currency.html)). New York exempts "self-custody wallets that don't hold others' funds", but bars licensees from acting "through an agent or agency arrangement when the agent is not a Licensee" ([NYDFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/apps_and_licensing/virtual_currency_businesses); [23 NYCRR 200](https://www.skadden.com/-/media/files/publications/2018/10/the-distributed-ledger-october-2018/fn26_dfsp200t.pdf)). California's Digital Financial Assets Law (DFAL) makes control the trigger and lists no exemption for agents of a licensee ([Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/blog/california-dfal-july-2026-licensing-deadline)).

Abroad, Canadian securities staff treat a custodial platform that gives users only a book-entry claim as trading securities or derivatives ([CSA Staff Notice 21-327](https://asc.ca/-/media/ASC-Documents-part-1/Regulatory-Instruments/2020/01/5795238-_-CSA-Staff-Notice-21-327.pdf)). Bermuda treats a licensee as the custodian even when it outsources custody ([BMA guidance note](https://cdn.bma.bm/documents/2025-09-08-11-57-23-Guidance-Note---Digital-Asset-Business---Guidance-for-prospective-applicants-for-licensing.pdf)). Switzerland keeps wallet providers that can dispose of client assets without the client's involvement out of its light settlement-account exemption ([Bär & Karrer](https://media.baerkarrer.ch/karmarun/image/upload/baer-karrer/Fintech_rules_eased.pdf)). **In every jurisdiction studied, a front end that holds the only key is treated as a custodian.**

The fix is narrow and mostly engineering. **Make the user's key required for every send**, with the user's passkey or device key acting either as the sole signer or as half of a 2-of-2 with the platform key; a platform key that can sign alone must not exist. **Put the user's key in the governance quorum as well**, because in Dakota's model whoever governs a signer group can add or remove signers and change policies, and the default quorum is one ([Dakota signing guide](https://docs.dakota.xyz/documentation/signing-guide.md)). Design recovery so that Moneta cannot rebuild control on its own. **Settle every user-to-user payment on-chain, wallet to wallet, through Dakota**, so that Dakota screens each payment and the chain becomes the ledger. Finally, hold no internal balances that differ from on-chain holdings, and no omnibus wallet. Dakota's card product already assumes this design. Enabling a wallet for cards means registering "the customer's key" as a signer, and for the card terms "only the customer can accept. There is no API to accept on their behalf" ([Dakota card enablement](https://docs.dakota.xyz/documentation/cards/wallet-enablement.md); [Dakota card activation](https://docs.dakota.xyz/documentation/cards/activation.md)).

If Moneta wants to keep server-side signing for convenience, the alternative is to let a licensed entity hold control. Dakota's own documents conflict on whether that is on offer. Its Terms describe a three-shard "Dakota-Supported Wallet" in which Dakota holds one shard, while its docs say "we never have access to your private keys" ([Dakota Terms](https://www.dakota.xyz/terms); [Dakota Wallets](https://docs.dakota.xyz/documentation/wallets.md)). Only Dakota can say which model its platform clients' end users have. A written agency agreement with Dakota is a fallback for the fiat flows. Even then Moneta needs its own written AML programme ([FinCEN FIN-2016-G001](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/shared/FIN-2016-G001.pdf)), and the agency route does not work for crypto activity in New York.

Fixing this before launch matters more than the choice of licence. An unregistered MSB faces a civil penalty of **$5,000 per violation, with each day counted separately** ([31 U.S.C. 5330](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5330)). Operating without a required state licence is a federal crime under 18 U.S.C. 1960. DOJ's April 2025 memo deprioritises non-wilful cases, but it changes neither the law nor what FinCEN and the states can do ([Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/blockchain-blog/deputy-attorney-general-memorandum-ending-regulation-by-prosecution.html)). And the NMLS multistate programme disqualifies any applicant whose company, affiliates or control persons have **prior unlicensed activity** ([NMLS MMLA checklist](https://mortgage.nationwidelicensingsystem.org/knowledge/Products/nmls/stateresourcecenter/Shared%20Documents/Multistate%20MSB%20Licensing%20Program%20Phase%20One%20Checklist.pdf)).

The redesign clears four side problems as well. First, Moneta could truthfully tell users they control their funds. Plasma, built on Bridge, can answer "Who custodies my money? You do" because its users hold the keys ([Plasma](https://www.plasma.org/)); calling Moneta "non-custodial" while it holds the only key would be deceptive. Second, users could still withdraw through Dakota if Moneta failed. Third, after Synapse, regulators focused on banks' "lack of access to records" and on reconciliation ([Joint Statement](https://fdic.gov/joint-statement-third-party-deposit-products.pdf)), and that concern largely disappears when the chain is the record. Fourth, internal transfers of $3,000 or more would otherwise each need Travel Rule records ([FIN-2019-G001](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20CVC%20Guidance%20FINAL.pdf)).

## Five more issues follow Moneta down every path

### Dakota's public contract excludes consumers

Dakota's Platform Services Agreement says: **"You may only use the Services for commercial purposes. The Dakota Account cannot be used for personal, consumer, or household purposes"** ([Dakota Terms](https://www.dakota.xyz/terms)). Every end user also accepts the Lead Bank Funds Transfer Agreement. It calls the funding account "a commercial-purpose deposit account", and it states that "fund transfers through Dakota are not subject to Regulation E, including the error resolution procedures" ([Lead Bank FTA](https://www.dakota.xyz/funds-transfer)). Yet Dakota onboards individuals for purposes such as "storage of funds" and "making online payments" ([Dakota individual onboarding](https://support-platform.dakota.xyz/articles/4406934078-onboarding-your-individual-account-customers)). Its card docs also mention separate consumer card terms (`cards_consumer_tos`) ([Dakota card activation](https://docs.dakota.xyz/documentation/cards/activation.md)).

Until Dakota supplies consumer terms, names the bank that has approved a consumer programme and allocates Regulation E error resolution for the ACH, off-ramp and card flows, every Moneta consumer would be agreeing not to use the account for personal purposes. **Counsel should treat this as a go/no-go item.** Dakota's documents also disagree on its banks: the Trust page names Lead Bank and BMO, while the Terms name Lead Bank and Erebor ([Dakota Trust](https://www.dakota.xyz/trust); [Dakota Terms](https://www.dakota.xyz/terms)). The whole issue falls away for a business-only launch, which is the market Dakota's own marketing targets: "For companies that hold money for businesses" ([Dakota business banking](https://www.dakota.xyz/solutions/business-banking)).

### "Bank" and "neobank" are regulated words in the US and Canada

In the US the restrictions are set state by state. **Illinois** bars non-banks from using "bank", "banker" or "banking" where that risks misleading the public, with penalties of up to $10,000 per violation ([205 ILCS 5/46](https://my.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext?DocName=020500050K46)). California and Illinois made Chime stop calling itself a bank in 2021 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/05/05/chime-has-agreed-to-stop-using-the-word-bank-after-a-california-regulator-pushed-back/)). California's November 2024 consent order against Dora Financial required a "not a bank" disclosure next to any such term ([DFPI](https://dfpi.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Consent-Order-Dora-Financial-LLC.pdf)). New York restricts the words under its Banking Law; a DFS interpretation cites §132, though commentators disagree on the section ([NYDFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/legal/interpret/lo070514.htm)). Chime and Dora could fall back on "banking services provided by our partner bank". **A stablecoin wallet run through a money transmitter has no partner bank to point to.**

Canada is stricter. Bank Act s.983 bars a non-bank from using "bank", "banker" or "banking", in any language, to describe a business in Canada. The ban covers a name, a domain name, a trademark or a description of services ([Bank Act s.983](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/B-1.01/section-983.html)). OSFI has listed "Mobile Banking" as off-limits, and fines of up to C$5 million have been reported ([Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/insights/blogs/2017/07/if-youre-not-a-bank-stop-using-bank-words-to-describe-your-business-says-canadas-banking-regulator)). "Neobank" contains the word.

Federal rules apply on top. The FDIC bars *anyone* from implying that uninsured products are FDIC-insured, and treats material omissions as violations ([12 CFR 328.102](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/328.102)). It has used this rule against crypto firms, including Utoppia, a neobank offering crypto wallets ([FDIC](https://fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2022/pr22060.html); [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/fdic-cease-desist-orders-neobank-utoppia-deposit-insurance-crypto/646155/)). The GENIUS Act treats any claim that stablecoins are federally insured or government-guaranteed as a violation of 18 U.S.C. 709 ([GENIUS Act](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ27/html/PLAW-119publ27.htm)). The safer pattern is to call the product a "money app" or "wallet", to describe Moneta as "a financial technology company, not a bank", and to state next to every balance that USDC is not a deposit, is not FDIC-insured and may lose value.

### California and New York are closed to a key-holding front end

**New York** requires a BitLicense to store, hold or control virtual currency for others, or to receive it for transmission ([NYDFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/apps_and_licensing/virtual_currency_businesses); [InnReg](https://www.innreg.com/blog/bitlicense-new-york)). Licensees may not work through unlicensed agents. The **$5,000 application fee is non-refundable**, a bond or trust account of generally **at least $500,000** is required, and moving fiat needs a separate money-transmitter licence. NYDFS publishes no timeline, and practitioners estimate **12–24 months**. NYDFS advertising guidance of 12 November 2026 requires licensees to update their policies by 10 May 2027 and to diligence third-party promoters ([NYDFS](https://dfs.ny.gov/reports_and_publications/press_releases/pr20261112)). Any New York partner will therefore police Moneta's copy.

**California's** DFAL became mandatory on 1 July 2026. Firms that had filed a complete application by then may keep operating; everyone else had to stop serving California residents. **Moneta missed that window** ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/02/registration-under-california-digital-financial-assets-law-begins-march-9-law-takes-effect-july-1); [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/alerts-finance-dcb-california-dfal-license-application-open)). DFAL reaches anyone with control over digital assets for a California resident, including anyone present in the state for more than 183 days a year. It has no agent-of-licensee exemption and sits alongside the separate California Money Transmission Act. It reportedly requires a $7,500 fee, about $100,000 of tangible net worth, a $500,000 bond, an independent BSA review and a security programme assessed against the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 ([Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/blog/california-dfal-july-2026-licensing-deadline); [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/alerts-finance-dcb-california-dfal-license-application-open)). The status of California's June 2026 amendments, reported as SB 97, could not be verified.

Neither state is on Dakota's licence list, and Dakota "does not engage in money transmission in any state where it is not licensed or otherwise authorized to do so" ([Dakota Licenses](https://www.dakota.xyz/licenses)). **Unless Dakota shows it is authorised in California and New York, Path 1 must geo-block both.** Illinois comes next: digital-asset businesses serving its residents must register with IDFPR by 1 July 2027 ([IDFPR](https://idfpr.illinois.gov/about/innovation.html)).

### GENIUS Act dates make balance rewards the riskiest feature

The GENIUS Act takes effect on **18 January 2027**. The OCC's final rule of 21 October 2026 is the only finished implementing rule; nearly every other rule is still a proposal ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/11/occ-finalizes-genius-act-rulemaking); [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20261027-occ-finalizes-genius-act-rule-for-permitted-payment-stablecoin-issuers-easing-several-key-proposed-requirements-and-raising-new-issues)). From that date only permitted issuers may issue payment stablecoins in the US, and anyone who "knowingly participates" in unlawful issuance faces up to $1 million per violation. From **18 July 2028**, a digital-asset service provider (DASP) may not offer or sell a non-permitted stablecoin to anyone in the US ([GENIUS Act](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ27/html/PLAW-119publ27.htm)). Whether Moneta is a DASP turns on the same custody facts as above. The definition excludes self-custodial software interfaces; it does not exclude hosted front ends.

On yield, the statute bars issuers from paying it "solely in connection with the holding, use, or retention" of a stablecoin. The OCC dropped its proposed presumption against rewards paid by affiliates and partners. But it offers "no safe harbor, white-label carve-out, or de minimis exception" and reserves the right to act against evasion ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/11/occ-finalizes-genius-act-rulemaking)). Its preamble treats two arrangements as violations: an agent paying yield on the issuer's behalf, and a white-label partner passing on payments made for the issuer ([Hogan Lovells Cadwalader](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/occ-finalizes-its-genius-act-rule-part-1-permitted-activities-and-prohibition-on-yield)).

Dakota markets its RD token with the line "Balances held in RD earn rewards that you can keep or pass on to your customers" ([Dakota Issuance](https://www.dakota.xyz/issuance)). **Once RD's issuer is regulated, that is exactly the pattern the OCC flagged.** RD is also blocked in Florida, Georgia, Louisiana, New York, Texas and Washington ([Dakota Support](https://support-platform.dakota.xyz/articles/4271304007-why-is-rd-blocked-for-some-customers-state-restrictions)), and its issuer's GENIUS status is not public. Congress's fix, which would have allowed activity-based rewards, died when the CLARITY Act failed cloture 49–50 on 15 September 2026 ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/in-the-wake-of-clarity-acts-failure-agencies-move-forward-without-congressional-action-or-certainty/)). Canada's Stablecoin Act likewise bans yield paid "directly or through intermediaries" but allows rewards tied to use ([BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/11/stablecoin-era-canada-proposes-stablecoin-regulations-and-expands-the-payment-regulatory-framework)).

**The defensible design is rewards tied to card spend or payments, funded by Moneta and not calculated on idle balances.** Such rewards are reported on Form 1099-MISC, not 1099-DA ([IRS 1099-DA instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099da); [The Tax Adviser](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2026/mar/navigating-the-form-1099-da-reporting-maze/)).

### An offshore licence never covers US residents

Four layers of US law reach an offshore provider that serves US residents. First, federal law makes it a crime for any person to take deposits "to any extent whatever" unless chartered and examined under US federal or state law, and officers who knowingly participate face up to five years in prison ([12 U.S.C. 378](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/378)). The OCC has repeatedly flagged offshore "banks" that solicit US depositors online as unauthorised ([OCC Alert 1998-7](https://occ.gov/news-issuances/alerts/1998/alert-1998-7.html)). Second, a non-bank offshore wallet doing business "wholly or in substantial part within the United States" is an MSB. It must register with FinCEN and name a US agent "even if the foreign-located entity has no physical presence in the United States" ([FinCEN 2011](https://fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-clarifies-money-services-businesses-definitions-rule-includes-foreign); [FIN-2019-A002](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/advisory/2019-05-10/FinCEN%20Advisory%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf)). Third, state licensing follows the customer. OKX's Seychelles entity pleaded guilty to unlicensed money transmission in February 2025 and paid about **$505 million**, after marketing at US events and helping US users get around its geofence ([DOJ SDNY](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/okx-pleads-guilty-violating-us-anti-money-laundering-laws-and-agrees-pay-penalties); [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/federal-prosecution-cautions-crypto-industry-on-state-money-transmitter-laws)). KuCoin paid about $297 million and agreed to leave the US for at least two years ([DOJ SDNY](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/kucoin-pleads-guilty-unlicensed-money-transmission-charge-and-agrees-pay-penalties)). Fourth, under Visa's single-country BIN rule, cards for US residents must come from a US issuer ([Visa Rules](https://bo.review.visa.com/content/dam/VCOM/download/visa-rules-public.pdf)). Puerto Rico is no workaround. It counts as "the United States" for BSA purposes, and its international financial entities may serve only other such entities and "Foreign Persons outside of Puerto Rico" ([31 CFR 1010.100](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100); [OCIF draft regulation](https://docs.pr.gov/files/OCIF/AVISOS-PUBLICOS/Proposed%20Regulation_International%20Banking%20and%20Financial%20Entities.pdf)). **An offshore licence is a way to serve non-Americans and nothing more.**

## In the US, Dakota comes first, own licences second, a charter last

### Path 1: launching under Dakota's licences

If the redesign takes Moneta out of money-transmitter status, **Moneta files nothing with a financial regulator**; its filings go to Dakota and Dakota's banks. Dakota requires a formed corporation or LLC, and sole proprietorships are ineligible. Its KYB package asks for the legal name, EIN, formation and governance documents, registered and operating addresses, a NAICS code, a business description of 200–1,024 characters, expected volumes, and proof of source of funds such as a bank statement, SAFE or subscription agreement. Each applicant, control person and owner of 25% or more supplies ID, SSN and proof of address dated within the last 90 days ([Dakota platform onboarding](https://support-platform.dakota.xyz/articles/2518037375-platform-client-onboarding)). Dakota applies enhanced due diligence because "EDD is required if your business transacts on behalf of third parties" ([same](https://support-platform.dakota.xyz/articles/2518037375-platform-client-onboarding)). That step asks for the top five customer countries, whether Moneta verifies identity and monitors on-chain activity, and either a licence type or an "unregulated justification" of 200–1,024 characters. **Counsel's memo on Moneta's status belongs in that justification.** "Unregulated" is not credible while Moneta holds the keys.

End users then accept Dakota's E-Sign notice, its Terms, its privacy policies, the Lead Bank Funds Transfer Agreement and an accuracy attestation. If Moneta builds its own onboarding flow, it must present those documents and record the consents itself ([Dakota customer onboarding](https://docs.dakota.xyz/documentation/customer-onboarding.md)).

Dakota and its bank will probably ask for the due-diligence package that bank regulators described after Synapse, in their July 2024 joint statement and the Evolve consent order ([Joint Statement](https://fdic.gov/joint-statement-third-party-deposit-products.pdf); [Federal Reserve Evolve order](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/files/enf20240614a1.pdf)). The package starts with a business plan, flow-of-funds diagrams and three-year projections. It adds a compliance management system, and a written BSA/AML programme and risk assessment. It needs an OFAC policy that blocks Dakota's prohibited jurisdictions and keeps records for 10 years ([OFAC final rule](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-03-21/html/2025-04864.htm)). It covers complaint management with root-cause analysis and reporting, and marketing pre-approval. It requires daily reconciliation against Dakota's records and on-chain balances, and an information-security programme that covers key management. It finishes with business continuity and a wind-down plan, vendor oversight, fraud and dispute handling, insurance, customer agreements and audit rights. The agencies proposed in September 2026 to rescind the joint statement and lighten their third-party guidance. The 2023 guidance stays in force until a final version is adopted ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/insights/legal/banking-agencies-propose-to-replace-the-2023-third-party-risk-management-guidance-and-to-rescind-the-bank-fintech-joint-statement)). Bridge's public agreement previews what Dakota's private one will probably require: pre-approved marketing, forwarding of complaints, referral of suspicious activity and record-keeping ([Bridge](https://bridge.xyz/legal/developer-agreement)).

The card needs approvals in sequence: Dakota, then the issuing bank, then the network, then card-art proofs signed off by all three. Mastercard requires programme managers to be approved as the BIN sponsor's agent ([Marqeta](https://www.marqeta.com/resources/program-management-guide); [Mastercard Rules](https://www.mastercard.com/content/dam/mccom/shared/business/support/rules-pdfs/mastercard-rules.pdf)). Partner insurance benchmarks run at **US$1 million per occurrence** each for cyber, professional liability/E&O, general liability and commercial crime ([Synctera](https://docs.synctera.com/docs/rc-implementation-operational-resilience)). Counsel and Dakota should settle in writing whether the card is a debit card on an account or "prepaid access". That decides which participant is the provider of prepaid access, with its own FinCEN registration ([31 CFR 1010.100](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-A/section-1010.100)).

**Costs** here are legal, compliance and insurance, not regulatory capital. **Timing** depends on how fast Dakota agrees a consumer programme and how fast the redesign ships. One vendor estimates six to nine months to build a card programme ([Trio](https://trio.dev/card-issuing-program/)).

Three blockers remain. The first is Dakota's 20-jurisdiction footprint: AL, AZ, AR, DC, FL, IL, IA, KS, MD, MI, MS, NE, NH, NM, ND, OH, RI, SC, SD and WV ([Dakota Licenses](https://www.dakota.xyz/licenses)). The second is the commercial-only contract stack. The third is Dakota's pending OCC trust-charter application, filed 28 July 2026 and opposed by the Bank Policy Institute and the ICBA ([BPI](https://bpi.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/BPI-Comment-Letter-re-Dakota-NTB-Application-8-27-26.pdf); [ICBA](https://www.icba.org/w/icba-opposes-dakota-ridge-national-trust-bank-charter-application)). If it is approved, Moneta becomes a third party of a bank and faces bank-style vendor diligence.

### Obligations every US operating company carries regardless of licence

**Formation.** The routine steps are a Delaware certificate of incorporation, bylaws, board consents, founder stock agreements and 83(b) elections, an EIN, registration as a foreign corporation in each state where staff work, and Delaware's annual franchise report each 1 March ([Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/expert-insights/delaware-corporations-annual-franchise-report-and-tax-requirement)). Since FinCEN's final rule took effect on 14 August 2026, a new US company has **no beneficial-ownership (BOI) filing**. Banks and Dakota still collect owner data under their own due-diligence rules ([MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260820-fincen-ends-beneficial-ownership-reporting)). Clear the brand in trademark Classes 36 and 9 before designing card art; likelihood of confusion is the USPTO's most common ground for refusal ([USPTO](https://uspto.gov/trademarks/search/likelihood-confusion)).

**Privacy and security.** As a non-bank financial institution, Moneta needs a written information-security programme under the FTC Safeguards Rule ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftc-safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know)). That means a designated Qualified Individual, a written risk assessment, MFA and encryption, annual penetration testing or continuous monitoring, an incident response plan, an annual report to the board, and **notice to the FTC within 30 days of a breach affecting 500 or more consumers**. Moneta also needs a GLBA/Regulation P privacy notice. California exempts only data covered by GLBA, not the company, so a website privacy policy that meets CCPA standards is still needed ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/07/Where-is-the-GLBA-Entity-Level-Exemption-Two-More-State-Privacy-Laws)).

**Consumer law.** Electronic disclosures require an affirmative E-SIGN consent step that meets 15 U.S.C. 7001(c) ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001)). The debit card falls squarely under Regulation E. Whether Regulation E also covers USDC wallets and in-app transfers between users is an open question. Congress nullified the CFPB's payment-app supervision rule on 9 May 2025, and the CFPB withdrew its stablecoin interpretive rule on 15 May 2025 ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/cfpb-overdraft-and-digital-payment-rules-repealed); [Orrick](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2025-05-16/cfpb-withdraws-eft-interpretive-rule/)). The prudent course is to adopt Reg E-style error resolution voluntarily, which collides with the Reg E disclaimer in the Lead Bank agreement. The FCC's TCPA "revoke-all" consent rule applies from 31 January 2027 ([Burr & Forman](https://www.burr.com/telephone-consumer-protection-act/the-fcc-delays-effective-date-of-tcpa-revoke-all-rule-until-january-31-2027)).

**Tax.** Collect W-9s at onboarding, and enrol in IRS TIN Matching before 2027 to keep relief from backup withholding ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-provides-additional-transition-relief-for-brokers-who-are-required-to-file-information-returns-and-backup-withhold-on-certain-digital-asset-sales)). Agree in writing with Dakota who files Form 1099-DA. Redeeming USDC for USD counts as a "sale", but under the optional stablecoin method nothing is reported for a customer with $10,000 or less of proceeds a year ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099da)).

### Path 2: Moneta's own licences

**Federal registration.** A principal MSB files FinCEN Form 107 within 180 days of being established ([FR 2026-08363](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-08363.pdf); [31 U.S.C. 5330](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5330)). The form lists every owner, director and officer, the bank holding the transaction account, and an estimate of the next year's volume. It renews every two years, and must be filed again after a transfer of 10% of the voting or equity interest. The IRS examines MSBs for BSA compliance. Registration brings the four pillars of an AML programme (written policies, a BSA officer, training and independent testing), suspicious-activity and cash reporting, and the Travel Rule. FinCEN's April 2026 proposal would make a documented risk assessment mandatory ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/10/2026-07033/anti-money-laundering-and-countering-the-financing-of-terrorism-programs)).

**State licences through NMLS.** Under the multistate programme, Washington runs a shared Phase One review for 32 states, and each state then runs its own Phase Two ([NMLS MMLA checklist](https://mortgage.nationwidelicensingsystem.org/knowledge/Products/nmls/stateresourcecenter/Shared%20Documents/Multistate%20MSB%20Licensing%20Program%20Phase%20One%20Checklist.pdf)). Every owner of 10% or more and every executive (CEO, CFO, COO, CTO, CCO/BSA officer) files an individual form (MU2), with a credit report and FBI fingerprints. Non-US citizens who have not lived in the US for 10 years also need a third-party investigative background report, **which affects any Canadian founder**. Twelve states require a qualifying individual; Indiana and Maryland require two and three years of money-transmission experience respectively. Eighteen states require audited financials before the application can be submitted, although start-ups may instead file unaudited quarter-end financials, proof of where their capital came from, and two-year projections. Twenty states require surety bonds before filing. The uploads include a business plan, formation documents, a certificate of good standing dated within 60 days, ownership and management charts, a flow-of-funds document, the AML policy with its risk assessment and any independent review, the bank accounts used, and the FinCEN registration number. The disqualifiers are a headquarters outside the US, prior unlicensed activity, and certain convictions of control persons. **The headquarters rule matters if the group's parent will be Swiss or offshore.**

**Money and time.** The MTMA model sets tangible net worth at the greater of $100,000 or a percentage of total assets. It sets each state bond at the greater of $100,000 or average daily liability, capped at $500,000, and requires permissible investments equal to outstanding customer obligations ([CSBS MTMA](https://www.csbs.org/sites/default/files/2023-02/CSBS%20Money%20Transmission%20Modernization%20Act.pdf)). The NMLS checklist's fees for the 32 multistate states add up to about **$64,700**, of which Texas alone charges $10,000; New York and California are extra. Vendor all-in estimates range from about $175,000 in fees for 53 jurisdictions ([SuretyBonds.com](https://www.suretybonds.com/blog/?p=10871)) to **$572,530 for 51 jurisdictions, with $8,455,000 of bond face** ([Faisal Khan](https://faisalkhan.com/pricing/us-state-money-transmitter-license-pricing)). Each state takes from 4–6 weeks to 6–12 months or more ([Spark](https://www.spark.money/tools/stablecoin-money-transmitter-license-comparison)), and New York takes 12–24 months.

Path 2 is the only US route if Moneta insists on holding keys. It is a second-phase project for a funded company with a compliance team, not a launch plan.

### Path 6: a bank or trust charter later

The OCC has chartered crypto trust banks at a record pace. Circle, Ripple and Coinbase all received final approval in 2026 ([Stablecoin Insider tracker](https://stablecoininsider.org/occ-charter-tracker/)). OCC Corporate Decision #1392 shows the standard conditions. The bank must hold **$10 million of tier 1 capital**, at least half of it (and no less than $5 million) in liquid assets, plus **180 days of wind-down operating expenses**, also in liquid assets. Its business plan is locked for three years, and senior hires need OCC non-objection. The approval expires if capital is not raised within 12 months or the bank does not open within 18 ([OCC CD #1392](https://www.occ.gov/topics/charters-and-licensing/interpretations-and-decisions/2026/cd1392.pdf)). Non-depository trust approvals have taken a median of 134 days ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2026/4/nonbanks-dominate-bank-charter-boom-as-approval-timelines-shrink-100265630)). The application includes a business plan with projections, biographical and financial reports on all organisers and directors, fingerprints, and before opening a BSA programme, an information-security programme and an IT architecture review ([Duane Morris](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/bankinglaw/2026/09/10/applying-for-a-national-trust-charter-key-requirements-from-the-occ/)). **A trust charter still allows no consumer deposits or lending.**

A real consumer bank costs far more. Revolut's conditional national bank approval requires **at least $95 million** of paid-in capital, plus FDIC and Federal Reserve approvals ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/revolut-nabs-conditional-occ-charter/829703/)). OneMain's industrial loan company launches with $341 million ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/onemain-fdic-approves-ilc-charter-subprime-lender-cfpb-13-ag-lawsuit-ncrc/875075/)). Even a Wyoming special-purpose depository institution now needs $5 million ([WY SF0055](https://wyoleg.gov/2026/Enroll/SF0055.pdf)). The OCC now also denies weak applications. It refused Wise in July 2026 over AML deficiencies ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/occ-denies-wise-trust-charter-application-aml-deficiencies/826156/)), and the ICBA sued on 2 October 2026 to challenge crypto trust charters ([Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2026/10/05/icba-sues-occ-over-crypto-national-trust-bank-charters-a-significant-challenge-to-the-occs-chartering-authority/)).

A trust charter fits Moneta's institutional custody and treasury idea once it can fund $10 million of tier 1 capital plus six months of wind-down costs and has a seasoned BSA and IT team. Until then, the outcome of Dakota's own application matters more.

## Offshore licences serve non-US customers only, and a real bank starts at US$10 million

"Offshore" covers two different products with very different price tags. A **deposit-taking bank licence** in a credible centre now starts around US$10 million. A Puerto Rico international financial entity (IFE) needs **US$10M paid in, plus US$1M of unencumbered assets in local banks**, at least eight full-time staff in Puerto Rico and an independent director; the application fee is US$50,000 and renewal costs US$25,000 a year ([McConnell Valdés](https://www.mcvpr.com/newsroom-publications-IBE-IFE-Reform)). An ADGM bank needs US$10M of base capital ([ADGM FSRA guide](https://assets.adgm.com/download/assets/Getting%2BStarted%2BGuide%2Bwith%2BFSRA.pdf/a8c5b07a589a11efa1111ed8b674bcd6)). An El Salvador investment bank needs US$50M and may serve only sophisticated investors ([García Bodán](https://garciabodan.com/en/el-salvador-positions-itself-as-regional-financial-hub-new-investment-banking-law/)). A Cayman bank looks cheap on paper at CI$400,000, but an unrestricted Class B licence "is normally only granted to a branch or affiliate of an existing bank" ([Conyers](https://www.conyers.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Banks_and_Trust_Companies_Licensing_and_Regulation-CAY.pdf)). A **digital-asset licence** permits custody, transfers and sometimes stablecoin issuance, but not insured fiat deposits, and costs far less:

### What offshore applications require

The application dossier is much the same everywhere, and Bermuda's September 2025 guidance spells it out most explicitly ([BMA guidance note](https://cdn.bma.bm/documents/2025-09-08-11-57-23-Guidance-Note---Digital-Asset-Business---Guidance-for-prospective-applicants-for-licensing.pdf)). It starts with an ownership chart through every layer to the ultimate owners, plus personal and institutional questionnaires for controllers. It adds three-year projections with quarterly detail for the first year, a staffing plan across the three lines of defence, an outsourcing inventory and insurance quotes. It requires a wind-down plan that covers returning client assets, and AML and sanctions policies with a named compliance officer and reporting officer. Finally it expects **a penetration test before go-live, smart-contract audits** and a gap analysis against Bermuda's custody code. So the product must be largely built before the licence arrives. Puerto Rico's draft adds three years of audited financial statements and source-of-wealth evidence for every investor or controlling person ([OCIF draft](https://docs.pr.gov/files/OCIF/AVISOS-PUBLICOS/Proposed%20Regulation_International%20Banking%20and%20Financial%20Entities.pdf)). That requirement often surprises seed investors.

### Why a digital-asset licence banks better than an "offshore bank"

The real gate is credibility with partner banks, and the word "bank" makes it harder to pass. US banks must apply enhanced due diligence to any foreign bank holding an "offshore banking license", which the rules define as a licence that bars banking with locals or in the local currency ([31 CFR 1010.605](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.605); [31 CFR 1010.610](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.610)). They may not serve a shell bank that lacks a staffed, inspectable office ([31 CFR 1010.630](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.630)). Puerto Rico has had two IFE liquidations: Euro Pacific in 2022, and Banex in September 2026, where OCIF is investigating more than US$24 million that disappeared ([OCIF](https://ocif.pr.gov/Avisos%20Publicos/Euro%20Pacific%20International%20Bank%20-%20Comunicado%20de%20Prensa.pdf); [El Nuevo Día](https://www.elnuevodia.com/english/business/story/ocif-orders-the-liquidation-of-another-international-bank-in-puerto-rico/)). The precedents that work are Xapo, a full Gibraltar bank that takes USDC deposits and withdrawals, and Coinbase's non-US exchange in Bermuda ([PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/xapo-bank-becomes-the-first-fully-licensed-bank-to-enable-usdc-deposits-and-withdrawals-301778907.html)).

Whether Dakota would onboard an offshore Moneta entity is unknown. Dakota does onboard non-US businesses. It puts sub-clients that serve their own customers through enhanced due diligence, and it excludes "high risk businesses" restricted by its banks ([Dakota sub-clients](https://docs.dakota.xyz/documentation/sub-clients.md); [Dakota prohibited use](https://support-platform.dakota.xyz/articles/4510934426-dakota-prohibited-use-categories-businesses)).

### Running offshore with or without a US entity

The structure that holds up is a **hard split**. A US entity serves US persons, through Dakota or its own licences. A non-US entity, licensed where it actually operates, serves only non-US persons. It screens out US persons using KYC data (nationality, tax residence, address, phone, ID country and funding bank), not just IP geofencing. RedotPay is the current example. Its offshore cards exclude US residents, and its separate US subsidiary won its first state money-transmitter licence in August 2026, with more than 20 applications pending ([RedotPay](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/14254838-card-issuance-restrictions); [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/crypto-web3-and-cbdc/news/redotpay-obtains-its-first-us-money-transmitter-licence)). Treasury's August 2026 proposal would give a safe harbour for non-US customers only where the provider reasonably believes recipients are outside the US and avoids US-targeted advertising ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/08/us-treasury-proposes-genius-act-rules-for-stablecoin-issuance-offer-and-sale)).

Without a US entity, Moneta simply gives up its US customers. Having US founders run the offshore entity day to day also weakens its claim to be foreign-located.

The tax cost is real. The offshore entity must register with the IRS under FATCA, obtain a GIIN and report US account holders ([Treasury FATCA](https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/tax-policy/foreign-account-tax-compliance-act)). It also reports under the amended Common Reporting Standard (CRS 2.0), in force in most jurisdictions from 1 January 2026, which now covers e-money ([Regnology](https://www.regnology.net/en/resources/regulatory-topics/common-reporting-standard-crs-2-0-aeoi/)). And it reports crypto under the OECD's CARF framework, which starts exchanging data in 2027 for Cayman and Bermuda ([OECD CARF list](https://web-archive-storage.oecd.org/aemint-web-archive-prod/web-archive/c8/c84b4af3884e1acd4f5adfdeac958eb0098d421347c25e756fbc963a39135ab9.pdf)). **US founders owning more than 50% would control a controlled foreign corporation (CFC).** Its profits would be taxed to them currently under Subpart F or the new NCTI regime (GILTI, renamed and widened for tax years after 2025). They would also file Form 5471 every year, and FBARs for any company accounts they can sign on ([EY](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-1510-united-states-changes-to-gilti-fdii-and-beat-among-others-included-in-final-reconciliation-legislation-but-not-previously-proposed-remedy-for-unfair-foreign-taxes); [IRS FBAR](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar)).

## Switzerland and the EU fit the institutional pivot, not a 2027 consumer launch

**Switzerland today has three tiers.** The first is membership of a self-regulatory organisation (SRO) such as VQF or PolyReg. It covers AML-only activity such as crypto transfer, brokerage and custody, and third-party guides put admission at 3–6 months ([VQF](https://vqf.ch/en/141-inhalte-english/sro/sro-beitritt); [lawsupport-ch](https://lawsupport-ch.pages.dev/licensing/vasp-registration-switzerland/)). An SRO-only firm may hold client money only within the settlement-account exemption (no interest, passed on within 60 days) or a CHF 1 million sandbox ([Bär & Karrer](https://media.baerkarrer.ch/karmarun/image/upload/baer-karrer/Fintech_rules_eased.pdf)). The second tier is the **FINMA FinTech licence**. It caps deposits at CHF 100 million and requires capital of 3% of deposits, with a minimum of CHF 300,000. It also requires a Swiss registered office and Swiss business activity, and it gives clients no protection if the firm goes bankrupt ([FINMA](https://www.finma.ch/en/authorisation/fintech/fintech-bewilligung/); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/che/legal-updates/FinTech-Licenses-now-part-of-Switzerland-s-financial-law-framework)). Only four firms held one in October 2025 ([Loyens & Loeff](https://www.loyensloeff.com/insights/news--events/news/swiss-federal-council-proposes-finia-amendments-new-licences-for-payment-and-crypto-institutions-to-replace-fintech-regime/)). The third tier is a **bank licence**, which needs CHF 10 million and management from Switzerland ([FINMA](https://www.finma.ch/en/authorisation/banks-and-securities-firms/getting-licensed/banks/)). Issuing a Swiss stablecoin without a bank licence currently requires a bank default guarantee that meets FINMA Guidance 06/2024 ([FINMA](https://www.finma.ch/en/news/2024/07/20240726-m-am-06-24-stablecoins/)).

The new **payment-instrument-institution** and **crypto-institution** licences are only a draft. Consultation ran from 22 October 2025 to 6 February 2026 ([Federal Council](https://www.admin.ch/en/newnsb/x4TMWQ1SWofNoFx7XyHhY)). No dispatch to Parliament had been found by mid-November 2026, and minimum capital is left to a future ordinance ([achivx tracker](https://achivx.org/regulation/switzerland/); [SIF fact sheet](https://www.sif.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/dUviLBIEgTjT/Faktenblatt%20Stablecoins.pdf)). **Entry into force before 2028 looks unlikely.** The split matters for Moneta. The payment-instrument licence covers client fiat and *Swiss* stablecoins, but USDC, as a stablecoin issued abroad, falls under the crypto-institution licence ([SIF fact sheet](https://www.sif.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/dUviLBIEgTjT/Faktenblatt%20Stablecoins.pdf)). A Moneta-type platform would therefore need both, or a bank licence.

**The EU needs two licences for USDC accounts.** USDC is an e-money token, issued in the EU through Circle's French e-money institution ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240701820713/en)).

The first licence is a MiCA authorisation as a crypto-asset service provider (CASP) for custody, transfer and exchange. It needs class 2 capital of **€125,000** ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/minimum-capital-requirements-crypto-asset)). It also needs an EU registered office, effective management in the EU and at least one EU-resident director ([MiCA Art. 59](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/mica/mica-regulation/article-59/)). The regulator has 25 working days to confirm the file is complete, then 40 working days to decide ([MiCA Art. 63](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/mica/mica-regulation/article-63/)). The application covers governance, ICT security, segregation of client assets, complaints, custody policy and a three-year programme of operations ([ESMA Art. 62](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-62-application-authorisation-crypto)). All MiCA transitional periods ended by 1 July 2026 ([Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/insights/what-the-end-of-micas-transitional-period-means-for-crypto-businesses/)). The second is a payment or e-money licence. Since the EBA's tolerance period ended on 2 March 2026, transferring or holding e-money tokens for clients needs one, or appointment as agent of a licensed provider ([EBA opinion](https://eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-07/bde8c438-bb3f-4343-a158-f42b7afc9650/EBA%20Op%202025%2008%20%28No-Action%20letter%20on%20PSD2-MICA%20interplay%29.pdf); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/02/eba-issues-supervisory-priorities-as-psd2-mica-transition-period-for-emt-activities-ends)). An e-money institution (EMI) needs **€350,000** of initial capital, and vendor estimates put approval at 6–24 months depending on the member state ([Crassula](https://crassula.io/guides/licenses/electronic-money-institution/); [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/emi-licence-eu/)). PSD3, which will reshape these licences, is not yet law ([EP Legislative Train](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-an-economy-that-works-for-people/file-revision-of-eu-rules-on-payment-services)).

There is no Swiss–EU passport. The reverse-solicitation exemption is lost if the firm, an affiliate or an influencer solicits the client ([CMS Luxembourg](https://cms.law/en/lux/legal-updates/reverse-solicitation-regime-exempting-third-country-firms-from-micar-authorisation-requirements)), so a Swiss-anchored group needs a real EU subsidiary. If the UK matters, the FCA's crypto authorisation window closes on 28 February 2027 for firms that want permission when the regime goes live on 25 October 2027 ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/new-regime-cryptoasset-regulation/how-gateway-will-operate)).

The fastest compliant sequence starts now with a Swiss AG, SRO membership and a FINMA-licensed partner bank. In parallel, Moneta would launch in the EU as agent or distributor of an existing EMI/CASP while applying for its own EU licences. It would move to the new FINMA licences once they are enacted. That sequence suits institutional treasury clients. **It does nothing for US consumers.**

## Canadian staff alone can trigger Canadian registration

**FINTRAC registration.** FINTRAC treats any company that is incorporated in Canada, or that has a physical location or **employees** there, as having a Canadian place of business, and "client location does not determine MSB status" ([FINTRAC](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/msb-esm/msb-eng)). Signing USDC sends at a client's request is "transferring virtual currency". A Canada-based Moneta would therefore very likely need to register as a money services business even with only US customers. Registration is **free, must come before operating, and takes about three months** for most complete files. It needs criminal-record checks, no more than six months old, for the CEO, the directors and anyone owning or controlling 20% or more ([FINTRAC registration](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/msb-esm/register-inscrire/reg-ins-eng)). It brings a five-part compliance programme: a compliance officer, written policies, a documented risk assessment, training, and an effectiveness review every two years ([FINTRAC compliance programme](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/guidance-directives/compliance-conformite/Guide4/4-eng)). Moneta would also report large virtual-currency transactions (C$10,000 or more) and follow the travel rule. Since 26 March 2026, penalties reach **C$20 million per violation for an entity**, or 3% of global revenue ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/03/canada-aml-update-three-bills-make-major-amendments-to-the-pcmltfa)).

**RPAA registration.** The Retail Payment Activities Act uses the same place-of-business test, "regardless of where their end users are" ([Bank of Canada](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/core-functions/retail-payments-supervision/supervisory-framework-registration/)). Registration with the Bank of Canada costs C$2,500 (indexed to inflation) plus an annual assessment ([Bank of Canada fees](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/core-functions/retail-payments-supervision/retail-payments-supervision-supervisory-policies-and-guidelines/registration-application-fee/)). The Act currently covers fiat transfers, so it reaches the USD ACH legs, but not digital currencies. Draft regulations of 24 October 2026 would bring in stablecoins and custodial wallets from about fall 2027, at an estimated compliance cost of C$152,127 a year for each newly covered provider ([Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-10-24/html/reg1-eng.html)). **Whether remote employees of a US company create a Canadian place of business is the single most important structuring question for counsel.**

**Serving Canadian residents adds three more layers.** Quebec, through Revenu Québec, licenses money-services businesses. A platform that takes Canadian dollars and converts them to crypto needs both the currency-exchange and funds-transfer classes, with roughly 50 days' processing and a police security clearance for each covered person ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/f99b4747/money-services-businesses-don-t-forget-quebec-s-specific-requirements); [Revenu Québec](https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/sector-specific-measures/money-services-businesses-msbs/cryptoasset-services/)). Provincial securities law treats a custodial book-entry model as trading. Moneta would have to register as a crypto trading platform, partner with one, or seek case-specific relief, and the relief granted so far covers only immediate-delivery models, such as zerohash's in July 2026 ([Cassels](https://cassels.com/our-insights/stablecoins-different-route-osc-grants-exemptive-relief-from-registration-for-stablecoin-trading/)). USDC remains on the securities regulators' list of accepted stablecoins ([CSA](https://www.securities-administrators.ca/crypto-platforms-regulation-and-enforcement-actions/crypto-undertakings/#VRCA)). The federal Stablecoin Act received Royal Assent on 26 March 2026 and is targeted to take effect in fall 2027. Whether USDC can still be offered then depends on Circle registering or being exempted ([Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/05/canadas-stablecoin-act); [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-10-24/html/reg1-eng.html)).

**Ontario staff carry a residual risk even in the US-only case.** Staff there may be treated as "trading from" the province, because OSC Rule 32-505's exemption covers only US-registered dealers and advisers ([OSC](https://www.osc.ca/sites/default/files/pdfs/irps/rule_20150528_32-505_cp-broker-dealers-advisers.pdf)). A federal corporation also needs at least 25% resident-Canadian directors ([CBCA s.105](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-105.html)).

## Conclusion

The licensing question is really a custody question. Letting users hold the key that must sign, and settling transfers on-chain, amounts to choosing Dakota's licences, with a vendor-diligence burden and no regulatory capital. Keeping the key amounts to choosing Moneta's own licences in every state, plus a BitLicense and a DFAL licence, plus the Canadian, Bermudian or Swiss equivalents wherever Moneta operates. Framed that way, the "offshore bank" is the least efficient option. It needs the most capital and serves none of the customers the product now targets. It also adds controlled-foreign-corporation tax for US founders, FATCA and CARF reporting, and the enhanced due diligence that US banks must apply to any "offshore banking license".

The underused option is to start with businesses and institutions. That segment fits Dakota's commercial-only contracts as written, the Swiss and EU treasury idea, the credibility of Bermuda or ADGM, and an eventual OCC trust charter. US retail is the segment that runs into Dakota's contracts, the "bank" rules and the California–New York gap. The launch date also decides which rules are live on day one:

| Date | What takes effect |
|---|---|
| 18 Jan 2027 | GENIUS Act |
| 31 Jan 2027 | TCPA "revoke-all" consent rule |
| 28 Feb 2027 | UK FCA crypto authorisation window closes |
| 1 Apr 2027 | FDIC digital signage rules for partner banks |
| 10 May 2027 | NYDFS advertising policies due |
| 1 Jul 2027 | Illinois digital-asset registration deadline |
| Fall 2027 | Canada's stablecoin regime (target) |
| 18 Jul 2028 | GENIUS ban on offering non-permitted stablecoins to US persons |

Dakota's own charter decision is still pending. Approval would strengthen Moneta's institutional pitch, and it would also make Moneta a bank's third party.

## Next steps and questions for counsel and Dakota

**Next steps, in order**

1. Do not move real customer money through the current build. Ship the redesign first: the user's key required for transactions and governance, no unilateral recovery, user-to-user payments on-chain through Dakota, and no omnibus wallet or internal balances.
2. Choose the first customer segment (US consumers, US businesses or non-US customers) and decide where the team will sit. Canadian staff and a non-US parent each change the filings.
3. Form the entity. A Delaware C-corp is the likely choice, but counsel should check it against any Canadian or Swiss structure. Get the EIN, run trademark clearance in Classes 36 and 9, and drop "bank" and "neobank" from the name, domain and copy.
4. Retain US fintech counsel for a written regulatory-status memo covering MSB status, state money-transmitter, DFAL and BitLicense exposure, DASP status and Regulation E. That memo becomes Moneta's answer to Dakota's enhanced due diligence.
5. Send Dakota the questions below, and get its private platform agreement, consumer terms and state licence list in writing.
6. Build the compliance pack partners will ask for: AML/OFAC programme and risk assessment, information-security programme and key-management policy, complaint handling, marketing review, E-SIGN flow, privacy notices, wind-down plan and insurance quotes.
7. Limit sign-up to Dakota's licensed states, excluding California and New York. Block RD in Dakota's six restricted states, and block Dakota's prohibited jurisdictions.
8. Park balance-based rewards and RD until counsel classifies them. If rewards are needed, make them activity-based and funded by Moneta.
9. Treat offshore, Swiss/EU and charter licences as second-phase decisions tied to a chosen non-US or institutional segment. If the institutional pivot goes ahead, book a FINMA pre-assessment and price SRO membership plus an EU EMI-agent route.
