# Offshore banking and digital-asset licence options for a fiat + stablecoin neobank (status as of 2026-11-24)

Scope note: this covers offshore licence routes only. Whether an offshore entity may serve US residents is covered by another researcher and is noted here only in passing. This is research, not legal advice; items marked "counsel must decide" need a licensed lawyer in that jurisdiction. Figures from law-firm marketing pages or crypto media are flagged as secondary or unverified.

---

## 1. What does each offshore licence route permit, and what does it cost in capital, fees and local substance?

### Takeaway
There are two very different products here. A **real deposit-taking bank licence** in a credible offshore centre now costs **US$10M+ in paid-in capital** (Puerto Rico IFE US$10M plus US$1M in local unencumbered assets; ADGM Category 1 US$10M base capital; El Salvador investment bank US$50M) plus a resident team. A **digital-asset business / VASP licence** (Bermuda DABA, Cayman VASP, Bahamas DARE, Gibraltar DLT, ADGM FSRA virtual-asset permission, Dubai VARA, El Salvador DASP, Mauritius VAITOS) costs roughly **US$100k to US$2M in capital**. It allows custody, exchange, transfers and in some places stablecoin issuance, but it does **not** allow insured fiat deposits. For a pre-launch startup, the second product plus a partner bank for fiat is the only route that fits a normal seed budget. A Cayman unrestricted Class B bank licence is normally granted only to affiliates of existing banks.

### Cited Findings

#### Puerto Rico: International Financial Entity (IFE, Act 273-2012 as amended by Act 44-2024)
- Act 44-2024 (IFEs) and Act 45-2024 (IBEs) were signed 16 Feb 2024 and took effect 90 days later, on 16 May 2024. — [McConnell Valdés alert, 6 Mar 2024](https://www.mcvpr.com/newsroom-publications-IBE-IFE-Reform); [Act 44-2024 official English text](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/2-ingles/0044-2024.pdf)
- **Capital:** a de novo IFE must have the full **US$10 million paid-in capital** at licensing. Existing IFEs phase up to US$10M under capital plans approved by the Commissioner. Paid-in capital may not fall below 10% of deposits unless the deposits are insured, and the entity must always be "well capitalized." — [McConnell Valdés](https://www.mcvpr.com/newsroom-publications-IBE-IFE-Reform)
- **Unencumbered assets:** in addition to the capital, an IFE must hold unencumbered assets with local Puerto Rico banks. A de novo IFE needs at least **US$1 million**. Existing entities phase in from US$500k (2024-25) to US$1.5M (2027-28). — [McConnell Valdés](https://www.mcvpr.com/newsroom-publications-IBE-IFE-Reform)
- **Substance:** at least **8 full-time employees in Puerto Rico** with employer-paid annual AML/CFT training. IFEs need at least 2 compliance staff, a full-time compliance officer and an autonomous compliance function. Books, records and the main operations (management, accounting, compliance) must be run from the Puerto Rico principal office. The board needs at least one **independent director** with no economic, banking, family or legal ties to the entity. — [McConnell Valdés](https://www.mcvpr.com/newsroom-publications-IBE-IFE-Reform)
- **Permitted activities:** IFEs and IBEs may act as **custodians of virtual assets and currencies with the Commissioner's prior "no objection,"** but **may not operate as virtual currency or digital-asset exchanges**. Deposit-taking IBEs may face legal reserve requirements of up to 20% of demand deposits. — [McConnell Valdés](https://www.mcvpr.com/newsroom-publications-IBE-IFE-Reform)
- **Fees:** IFE application fee **US$50,000** (non-refundable, plus investigation costs above that). Annual licence renewal **US$25,000**, plus US$5,000 per additional office. Renewal requires a CEO affidavit and an auditor's report on BSA and OFAC compliance. — [McConnell Valdés](https://www.mcvpr.com/newsroom-publications-IBE-IFE-Reform)
- **OCIF draft implementing regulation (dated "May ___, 2025"; status: proposed):**
  - Permitted IFE services go to other IFEs or "**Foreign Persons outside of Puerto Rico**."
  - An IFE branch may not accept deposits.
  - A depository IFE may offer correspondent banking to foreign banks only with prior authorisation and a certification signed by the CEO and the BSA officer. It may never serve a "shell bank" as defined in federal law.
  - An IFE must commence operations within **180 days** of licensing.
  - Incomplete applications not cured within **30 days** are returned.
  - Source: [OCIF Proposed Regulation, English draft](https://docs.pr.gov/files/OCIF/AVISOS-PUBLICOS/Proposed%20Regulation_International%20Banking%20and%20Financial%20Entities.pdf)
- **Who may apply:** only non-individual entities organised under Puerto Rico, US, state or territorial law may operate as IFEs. — [McConnell Valdés](https://www.mcvpr.com/newsroom-publications-IBE-IFE-Reform)
- **Crypto precedent:** FV Bank is an OCIF-licensed IFE that launched digital-asset custody and settlement, holding crypto and fiat in one account. — [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/puerto-rico-fv-bank-digital-asset-custody/636277/). In Dec 2022 FV said it was on track to open a Federal Reserve Bank of New York master account. — [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2022/12/puerto-rico-chartered-crypto-bank-expects-to-open-fed-master-account-73067739). I found no confirmation that the account was ever opened.

#### Cayman Islands: Class A / Class B bank licence and the VASP Act
- **Bank licence categories:** Category "A" and "B" licences, each available to a branch or a locally incorporated company. Restricted Category "B" licences exist, as do trust licences. Locally incorporated applicants must give an undertaking not to solicit funds in the Cayman Islands. Branches must give an undertaking not to trade in the Cayman Islands. — [CIMA Banking Services Licensing Requirements](https://cima.ky/banking-services-regulatory-requirements)
- **Capital:** the minimum for a Class A, Class B or trust licence is **CI$400,000 (about US$480,000)**. Restricted Class B licences need a minimum net worth of **CI$20,000**. — [Conyers guide](https://www.conyers.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Banks_and_Trust_Companies_Licensing_and_Regulation-CAY.pdf); [Ogier](https://ogier.com/news-and-insights/insights/licensing-and-ongoing-requirements-of-banks-in-the-cayman-islands). CIMA's minimum capital-adequacy ratios are **15% for locally incorporated banks** and 12% for subsidiaries of consolidated-supervised banks. — [CIMA](https://cima.ky/banking-services-regulatory-requirements)
- **Practical gate:** an unrestricted Class B licence "is normally only granted to a branch or affiliate of an existing bank operating in a well regulated jurisdiction." — [Conyers](https://www.conyers.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Banks_and_Trust_Companies_Licensing_and_Regulation-CAY.pdf)
- **Physical presence:** CIMA's 2018 criteria expect premises at a Cayman physical address from which business is conducted, with exterior identification. — [CIMA physical-presence criteria](https://www.cima.ky/upimages/commonfiles/SOG-CriteriaforMaintainingPhysicalPresenceforBanks_1515699493.pdf). A Conyers guide says a B licence "may be conducted from offices in Cayman though that is not a requirement" ([Conyers](https://www.conyers.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Banks_and_Trust_Companies_Licensing_and_Regulation-CAY.pdf)). This conflicts with CIMA's criteria; CIMA is the more authoritative source.
- **Processing time:** CIMA states about **6–8 weeks for a complete application**. — [CIMA](https://cima.ky/banking-services-regulatory-requirements)
- **VASP Act phase two (in force 1 April 2025):** entities providing **virtual-asset custody** or operating **virtual-asset trading platforms** must hold a CIMA **licence**; other VASP activities need registration. — [CIMA VASP FAQ](https://www.cima.ky/vasp-faq); [Walkers, Mar 2025](https://www.walkersglobal.com/en/Insights/2025/03/Cayman-Islands-licensing-regime-for-virtual-asset-custody-providers-and-trading-platform-operators). Existing registrants had a 90-day window to apply, to about 29 June 2025. — [Carey Olsen](https://www.careyolsen.com/node/5110). CIMA updated its regulatory policy on registration and licensing in May 2025. — [CIMA Regulatory Policy, 27 May 2025](https://www.cima.ky/upimages/regulatorymeasures/RPy-RegistrationorLicensingofVASPs-27-May-25_1748960021.pdf)
- At its November 2025 VASP forum, CIMA described its application processing as rigorous and warned that firms often misclassify activities under the Money Services Act. — [CIMA 2025 VASP Forum highlights](https://www.cima.ky/upimages/commonfiles/2025VASPRegulatoryForumSummaryHighlights-November2025_1764177435.pdf)

#### Bermuda: Digital Asset Business Act (DABA) Class T/M/F and banking licence
- **Classes:** "T" (test or pilot), "M" (modified or transitional) and "F" (full). — [BMA Digital Asset Business page](https://bma.bm/digital-asset-business). Class F has no expiry but is subject to regular supervisory visits. — [BMA](https://bma.bm/digital-asset-business)
- **Application fees:** T licence US$1,000; M licence US$2,266. — [BMA Information Bulletin, Feb 2024](https://www.bma.bm/viewPDF/documents/2024-02-21-11-17-19-Information-Bulletin-DAB-Application-ProcessFebruary-2024.pdf). A 2026 secondary source gives US$2,266 for both M and F. — [Coincub](https://coincub.com/blog/bermuda-daba-licensing/) (secondary)
- **Minimum net assets:** US$10,000 for Class T and US$100,000 for Class M and F, and the BMA can require more. — [Coincub](https://coincub.com/blog/bermuda-daba-licensing/) (secondary; not confirmed against the BMA rules)
- **BMA capital expectation (primary):** Class F applicants must "propose and justify their capital model" covering the full spectrum of their risks. All applicants must show sufficient net assets and liquidity to withstand market volatility. — [BMA Guidance Note for prospective DAB applicants, 8 Sep 2025](https://cdn.bma.bm/documents/2025-09-08-11-57-23-Guidance-Note---Digital-Asset-Business---Guidance-for-prospective-applicants-for-licensing.pdf)
- **Substance (primary):**
  - Applicants must give a staffing breakdown across the three lines of defence, stating which roles are Bermuda-based and which are outsourced or insourced, and the % of time non-Bermuda staff give to Bermuda.
  - They must show how they will meet the BMA **Head Office** requirements. Class T is exempt but should present a roadmap toward meeting them.
  - Class T needs evidence of **at least two qualified directors**.
  - Source: [BMA Guidance Note, Sep 2025](https://cdn.bma.bm/documents/2025-09-08-11-57-23-Guidance-Note---Digital-Asset-Business---Guidance-for-prospective-applicants-for-licensing.pdf)
- **Stablecoin issuers (primary):**
  - The BMA wants a reserve investment policy of high-quality liquid assets (short-term government securities, bank deposits, public money-market funds) with value at least equal to the tokens in circulation.
  - It wants daily liquidity sufficient to redeem at par.
  - It wants a named third-party attestation firm and a named audit firm.
  - Multi-chain issuers must explain whether issuance is bridged or native and how proof of reserves works across chains.
  - Source: [BMA Guidance Note §4.3](https://cdn.bma.bm/documents/2025-09-08-11-57-23-Guidance-Note---Digital-Asset-Business---Guidance-for-prospective-applicants-for-licensing.pdf)
- **Custody:** applicants must give a gap analysis against the Digital Asset Custody Code of Practice 2024 **even if custody is outsourced**, because the licensee is still regarded as the custodian. — [BMA Guidance Note](https://cdn.bma.bm/documents/2025-09-08-11-57-23-Guidance-Note---Digital-Asset-Business---Guidance-for-prospective-applicants-for-licensing.pdf)
- **Bank licence precedent:** in June 2022 Jewel Bank received a **full banking licence and a Class F DAB licence**. It was described as "Bermuda's first new bank in 21 years." Its planned services were core banking, digital-asset custody, traditional and crypto-collateralised lending, fiat on/off-ramps, and stablecoin issuance and redemption, aimed at fintechs. The government noted that Bermuda's 15 licensed fintechs were "all banked overseas." — [Government of Bermuda press release, 8 Jun 2022](https://www.gov.bm/news/premier-minister-finance-congratulates-jewel-bank)
- Operational resilience: Class F firms must implement the BMA operational-resilience framework by 31 March 2028. — [Coincub](https://coincub.com/blog/bermuda-daba-licensing/) (secondary)

#### The Bahamas: DARE Act 2024 (Securities Commission) and bank/trust licence (Central Bank)
- The **Digital Assets and Registered Exchanges Act 2024** replaced the DARE Act 2020. Existing registrants had 90 days to re-apply. — [Chambers Fintech 2026, Bahamas](https://chambers.com/practice-guides/guides/fintech-2026/bahamas/law-and-practice); [gazetted Act](https://www.scb.gov.bs/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Gazetted-Digital-Assets-and-Registered-Exchanges-Act-2024.pdf); [SCB media release, Jul 2024](https://www.scb.gov.bs/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Media-Release-The-Bahamas-Introduces-Transformative-Digital-Asset-Legislation-The-DARE-Act-2024.pdf)
- **Registrable activities:** exchange operation, fiat/crypto conversion, payment services, order execution, **stablecoin issuance**, custody, advice, management, DLT node services, derivatives and staking. — [Chambers 2026](https://chambers.com/practice-guides/guides/fintech-2026/bahamas/law-and-practice)
- **Stablecoins:**
  - Algorithmic stabilisation is banned.
  - Stablecoins must be fully backed with segregated reserves, quarterly proof-of-reserves reporting, independent audits and 1:1 redemption.
  - The Commission can halt or delist a stablecoin.
  - Issuers must verify a holder's identity before redeeming.
  - Source: [Chambers 2026](https://chambers.com/practice-guides/guides/fintech-2026/bahamas/law-and-practice)
- **Capital:** there is no fixed figure in the sources. The SCB sets regulatory capital and insurance "in proportion to the activities." — [Chambers 2026](https://chambers.com/practice-guides/guides/fintech-2026/bahamas/law-and-practice); [DARE Bill 2023 consultation](https://www.scb.gov.bs/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/DARE-Bill-2023-Consultation-Document.pdf). Registered exchanges must hold **assets in The Bahamas equal to at least 12 months of operating expenses**. — [Chambers 2026](https://chambers.com/practice-guides/guides/fintech-2026/bahamas/law-and-practice)
- **Reach:** providing digital-asset services to Bahamas residents from abroad also requires registration, unless the client is an accredited investor. — [Chambers 2026](https://chambers.com/practice-guides/guides/fintech-2026/bahamas/law-and-practice)
- **Bank and trust licence (Central Bank):** every applicant must meet the Central Bank's physical-presence guidelines (premises, qualified staff, local decision-making). "Managed" licensees that meet the managed-licensee criteria are exempt. — [Central Bank physical-presence guidelines, rev. Apr 2014](https://www.centralbankbahamas.com/viewPDF/documents/2019-06-26-07-40-18-Physical-Presence-Guidelines-Revised-April-2014.pdf); [Central Bank general licensing information](https://centralbankbahamas.com/bank-supervision/general-information/general-information-and-guidelines-for-licence-applications-for-banks-and-or-trust-companies)
- **Bank capital:** older summaries conflict, giving US$1,000,000 or US$2,000,000 for a public bank and/or trust. The Central Bank sets capital case by case. — [Mondaq (older)](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/offshore-financial-centres/3875/establishing-a-bank-andor-trust-company-in-the-bahamas). The Central Bank issued new Banks and Trust Companies regulations in 2025. — [Central Bank, Regulations 2025](https://www.centralbankbahamas.com/index.php/viewPDF/documents/2025-11-05-12-45-34-Banks-and-Trust-Companies-Private-Trust-Companies-and-Qualified-Executive-Entities-Regulations-2025.pdf)
- All-digital banks are expected possibly within 12–36 months of the Chambers 2026 publication. This is not yet a regime. — [Chambers 2026](https://chambers.com/practice-guides/guides/fintech-2026/bahamas/law-and-practice)

#### British Virgin Islands: VASP Act
- The BVI has passed a VASP Act and issued enhanced technical guidance on VASPs. — [Government of the Virgin Islands, 2025 jurisdiction update](https://gov.vg/news/risa-bvi-south-square-conference-2025-jurisdiction-update)
- The FATF added the BVI to its increased-monitoring ("grey") list on **13 June 2025**. — [Maples](https://maples.com/knowledge/the-british-virgin-islands-and-the-fatf-monitoring-list); [Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cmc0coiay00byuzu4dwhs0bst/british-virgin-islands-added-to-the-fatf-aml-grey-list). It was still listed after the October 2026 plenary (see section 3).
- The EU added the BVI to its AML high-risk third-country list via Delegated Regulation (EU) **2026/83**, effective **29 January 2026**. — [EUR-Lex 2026/83](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/83/oj); [Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/eu-list-of-highrisk-countries-and-jurisdictions-for-money-laundering-and-terrorism-financing-amended)
- Tether announced in January 2025 that it would move its headquarters **from the BVI** to El Salvador after obtaining Salvadoran licences. — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/crypto/tether-to-relocate-hq-to-el-salvador-following-license-approval)

#### Gibraltar: DLT Provider licence and banking licence (Xapo precedent)
- The DLT Provider licence is a Part 7 permission granted by the GFSC under the Financial Services Act 2019. The governing DLT regulations were last consolidated on 27 Oct 2025 and 26 Mar 2026. — [Financial Services (DLT Providers) Regulations 2020, consolidated](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/financial-services/2020s012/2020s012%2827-10-25%29.pdf); [GFSC DLT scope guidance, 28 Mar 2025](https://www.fsc.gi/uploads/Guidance%20Notes/Guidance%20Note%20-%20Scope%20of%20the%20DLT%20Framework%20%28Final%2028.03.2025%29.pdf)
- **Fees:**
  - The GFSC's 2025/26 fees newsletter raises the DLT annual trade-activity fee cap from £75,000 to **£90,000** and sets an additional annual fee of **£27,362**. — [GFSC Fees Newsletter 2025/26](https://www.fsc.gi/uploads/GFSC%20Fees%20Newsletter%202025_2026.pdf)
  - A commercial guide cites a **£45,000 application fee**, a £2,000 initial assessment fee and a £12,254 annual base fee for 2026/27. — [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/news/crypto-license-in-gibraltar-gfsc-dlt-provider-licence-2026-2027-guide-1538756) (marketing source; unverified)
- **Capital:** there is no fixed statutory minimum. The GFSC expects financial resources proportionate to the business model and risk. — [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/dlt-provider-licence-gibraltar/) (secondary)
- A 2026 amendment requires a provider to notify the GFSC, and receive no objection, before paying dividends or capital distributions. — [Gibraltar DLT Regulations, amendment](https://gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/financial-services/2020s012/2025=040.pdf) (as summarised in search results; not read in full)
- **Xapo Bank:** a Gibraltar-licensed bank.
  - It received its GFSC banking licence after relocating from Switzerland. — [Decrypt, 2020](https://decrypt.co/54719/bitcoin-custodian-xapo-set-to-receive-banking-license-in-gibraltar); [finews.com](https://www.finews.com/news/english-news/68507-xapo-bank-silicon-valley-switzerland-gibraltar-crypto-bitcoin-private-banking-seamus-rocca-wences-casares)
  - Deposits are covered by the Gibraltar Deposit Guarantee Scheme up to the USD equivalent of €100,000. — [Xapo, "Is Xapo a regulated bank?"](https://xapo.com/resources/is-xapo-a-regulated-bank)
  - In March 2023 it became the first "fully licensed bank" to accept USDC deposits and withdrawals, converting USDC 1:1 to USD. — [PR Newswire, Mar 2023](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/xapo-bank-becomes-the-first-fully-licensed-bank-to-enable-usdc-deposits-and-withdrawals-301778907.html)
  - It is described as a principal member of Visa and Mastercard. — [Bizcommunity press office](https://www.Bizcommunity.com/PressOffice/AboutUs.aspx?i=623282) (company-supplied; weak source)
  - It reportedly offered UK clients interest-bearing bitcoin accounts via passporting of its licence. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/309323/xapo-bank-uk-bitcoin)

#### UAE: Abu Dhabi ADGM (FSRA) and Dubai VARA
- **ADGM bank (Category 1):** accepting deposits carries a **US$10 million base capital requirement**, plus separate credit, market, operational and CVA risk capital. — [ADGM FSRA Getting Started Guide](https://assets.adgm.com/download/assets/Getting%2BStarted%2BGuide%2Bwith%2BFSRA.pdf/a8c5b07a589a11efa1111ed8b674bcd6). A "digital bank" in ADGM accepts deposits and must meet Category 1 prudential requirements. — [ADGM Digital Bank Authorisation Guide](https://assets.adgm.com/download/assets/ADGM+Digital+Bank+Authorisation+Guide+2019+%281%29.pdf/ed553d387b1211efb0ca3abe06ddaef9)
- **ADGM virtual-asset custody:** listed in Category 3C. The base capital is expressed as 26/52 of annual audited expenditure. — [FSRA Getting Started Guide](https://assets.adgm.com/download/assets/Getting%2BStarted%2BGuide%2Bwith%2BFSRA.pdf/a8c5b07a589a11efa1111ed8b674bcd6). A June 2025 news report says the requirement is US$250,000 or six months' audited operating expenses. — [The National, 11 Jun 2025](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2025/06/11/adgm-crypto-rules/). These conflict; check the FSRA PRU rulebook.
- **ADGM stablecoins (fiat-referenced tokens, FRTs):**
  - The FRT issuance framework was introduced in December 2024 with par-redemption rights. — [ADGM announcement](https://www.adgm.com/media/announcements/fsra-introduces-a-regulatory-framework-to-support-the-issuance-of-fiat-referenced-tokens-in-adgm)
  - The FRT issuer **base capital requirement is US$2,000,000** (CET1, or annual audited expenditure if higher), held separately from reserves. — [CMS on FSRA consultation](https://cms.law/en/are/legal-updates/UAE-FSRA-consults-on-proposed-stablecoin-regulatory-framework-for-ADGM)
  - Amendments to the FRT framework were finalised **31 October 2025**. — [ADGM announcement](https://www.adgm.com/media/announcements/adgm-fsra-finalises-regulatory-framework-for-regulated-activities-involving-fiat-referenced-tokens)
  - A 2025 package also revised capital requirements and fees for virtual-asset firms. — [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/adgm-fsra-implements-amendments-to-its-digital-asset-regulatory-framework)
- **Dubai VARA paid-up capital (Company Rulebook Part VI, version 19 May 2025):** each amount is the greater of the AED figure or a % of fixed annual overheads.
  - Custody: AED 600,000 or 25% of overheads.
  - Broker-dealer: AED 400,000 or 15% (using a VARA-licensed custodian), otherwise AED 600,000 or 25%.
  - Exchange: AED 800,000 or 15% (with a licensed custodian), otherwise AED 1,500,000 or 25%.
  - VA transfer and settlement: AED 500,000 or 25%.
  - Advisory: AED 100,000.
  - Net liquid assets must be at least 1.2x monthly operating expenses.
  - Reserve assets must equal 100% of client liabilities, held 1:1 in the same virtual asset.
  - A firm licensed for several activities must hold the capital for each activity separately.
  - Source: [VARA Rulebook Part VI](https://rulebooks.vara.ae/entiresection/110); [VARA licence applications](https://vara.ae/en/licenses-and-register/licence-applications)
- VARA application fees of AED 40k or 100k per activity, plus annual supervision fees, are cited only by secondary guides. — [Cryptoverse Lawyers](https://www.cryptoverselawyers.io/vara-paid-up-capital-licensing-fees/) (unverified)

#### El Salvador: CNAD DASP (PSAD) registration and the 2025 Investment Banking Law
- **Investment Banking Law:**
  - The Assembly approved it on 7 Aug 2025 (55 of 60 votes) and it was published in the Official Gazette on 11 Aug 2025. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/366241/el-salvadors-new-bitcoin-law-signals-shift-from-retail-adoption-to-institutional-investment); [Consortium Legal](https://consortiumlegal.com/en/2025/08/21/investment-bank-law-in-el-salvador/)
  - **Minimum capital is US$50 million**, paid in dollars and deposited with the BCR (central bank), with at least two shareholders. — [García Bodán](https://garciabodan.com/en/el-salvador-positions-itself-as-regional-financial-hub-new-investment-banking-law/); [Consortium Legal](https://consortiumlegal.com/en/2025/08/21/investment-bank-law-in-el-salvador/)
  - Services are limited to **sophisticated investors** with over US$250,000 in liquid assets. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/366241/el-salvadors-new-bitcoin-law-signals-shift-from-retail-adoption-to-institutional-investment)
  - With a PSAD licence, these banks may conduct investment, financing and custody with bitcoin, stablecoins and tokenised assets. — [García Bodán](https://garciabodan.com/en/el-salvador-positions-itself-as-regional-financial-hub-new-investment-banking-law/); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitcoin-investment-banks-coming-el-salvador)
  - The Superintendencia del Sistema Financiero supervises them, and the BCR was to issue technical standards within 60 days. — [Consortium Legal](https://consortiumlegal.com/en/2025/08/21/investment-bank-law-in-el-salvador/)
- **CNAD stablecoin-issuer registration:** an issuer needs at least one CNAD-approved issuance, a PSAD registration for placing and marketing the stablecoin, and a report from a CNAD-registered certifier. — [CNAD "How to register"](https://cnad.gob.sv/?p=3933). A CNAD requirements document also covers issuer capital and audited financial statements. — [CNAD issuer requirements, via RegAlert](https://regalert.today/document/requirements-for-stablecoin-issuers-and-issuance-0e458091-27dc-4c35-85d0-d428122184ab/text)
- **PSAD (DASP) process:** online pre-registration, then a favourable or unfavourable resolution; unfavourable applications can be resubmitted. — [CNAD](https://cnad.gob.sv/?p=3914). CNAD publishes a public register of PSAD holders, many of them registered for exchange. — [CNAD register](https://cnad.gob.sv/?p=10722)
- **Fees and timelines:** a "USD 2,500 PSAD AML verification fee" and about 20 business days for pre-registration are cited only by third-party guides. — [RegAlert guide](https://regalert.today/guides/fintech/SV); [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/news/bitcoin-service-provider-licence-el-salvador) (unverified)

### Inferences
- **The "offshore bank" goal and the startup budget conflict.** Every credible full-bank route found needs at least US$10M of paid-in capital plus local staff and an independent board: Puerto Rico IFE US$10M + US$1M local assets + 8 FTE; ADGM Cat 1 US$10M; El Salvador investment bank US$50M and sophisticated investors only. Cayman's unrestricted Class B is low-capital on paper but in practice limited to affiliates of established banks. Bahamas bank capital is low on paper (US$1–2M in older sources) but physical-presence rules apply and no new-entrant digital-bank regime exists yet.
- **The realistic offshore route for a seed-stage fiat + stablecoin product is a digital-asset licence plus a partner bank for fiat.** The candidate licences are Bermuda DABA (Class M, then F), ADGM FSRA (VA permission, optionally FRT), Dubai VARA, Cayman VASP or Bahamas DARE. This mirrors the current Dakota model, with the licensed digital-asset entity offshore instead of a US MSB.
- **Activity fit:**
  - A Puerto Rico IFE can custody virtual assets (with no objection) but **cannot run an exchange**. A product that swaps fiat and USDC for users would need careful structuring or a third-party exchange or on-ramp. Counsel must decide.
  - Bermuda, the Bahamas, ADGM and El Salvador explicitly contemplate **stablecoin issuance**. That matters only if Moneta ever issues its own token, which is not the current plan (it holds USDC).
- **Puerto Rico is the only listed option that is US-jurisdiction:** a US territory, US BSA/OFAC applies, and IFEs are organised under PR/US law. The draft regulation restricts services to "Foreign Persons outside of Puerto Rico," so it is not a vehicle for serving US residents. The other researcher should confirm this.

## 2. What application dossier do these regulators typically require?

### Takeaway
The dossier is broadly the same everywhere and is heavy. It covers:
- an ownership chart through every layer to the ultimate beneficial owners, with personal questionnaires, police certificates and references for controllers, directors and officers;
- audited financials of controllers, proof of capital and source of funds and wealth;
- a business plan with 2–3-year projections;
- AML/CFT manual and business risk assessment, plus named compliance officer and MLRO/reporting officer;
- a cyber and IT security framework with penetration-test results;
- outsourcing agreements and due diligence;
- insurance quotes;
- wind-down plans;
- auditor consent;
- a local registered or principal office.

Bermuda's Sept 2025 Guidance Note and Puerto Rico's 2025 draft regulation are the most explicit published checklists found.

### Cited Findings
- **Bermuda (BMA Guidance Note, 8 Sep 2025):**
  - an ownership chart showing all intermediate layers to the UBOs with percentages, plus all affiliates and their regulatory status;
  - **Personal Questionnaires** and **Institutional Questionnaires** for shareholder controllers;
  - **3-year financial projections with quarterly detail for the first 12 months** (revenue, cash burn, capital adequacy) and a capital-structure table;
  - disclosure of funding sources;
  - staffing across the three lines of defence and a staffing roadmap;
  - an outsourcing inventory with due diligence, SLAs and contingency plans;
  - insurance (E&O/PI, D&O, crime) with executed contracts or quotes;
  - **wind-down plans** (Class F plans must address client-asset return);
  - graduation plans for Class T/M;
  - AML/ATF and sanctions policies, a business risk assessment, and named Compliance Officer and Reporting Officer with evidence of competence in Bermuda AML law;
  - an internal-audit framework;
  - cyber evidence: annual external pen testing, **pen test before go-live** with the summary given to the BMA, monthly external and six-monthly internal vulnerability scanning, a secure development life cycle, and **smart-contract security audits** before production;
  - a custody-code gap analysis.
  - Source: [BMA Guidance Note](https://cdn.bma.bm/documents/2025-09-08-11-57-23-Guidance-Note---Digital-Asset-Business---Guidance-for-prospective-applicants-for-licensing.pdf)
- **Puerto Rico (OCIF draft regulation, May 2025):**
  - **audited financial statements for each of the 3 prior years** of every investor or controlling person;
  - identity and background (full name, address, marital status, SSN or equivalent, passport number) of each proposed director, executive officer and the **BSA Compliance Officer**;
  - evidence of paid-in capital and unencumbered assets **and their source of funds and source of wealth** (bank statements and similar);
  - identity and justification of the **Independent Director**;
  - a Personal History Form, with the Commissioner investigating criminal records;
  - articles naming the principal office in Puerto Rico.
  - Source: [OCIF draft regulation](https://docs.pr.gov/files/OCIF/AVISOS-PUBLICOS/Proposed%20Regulation_International%20Banking%20and%20Financial%20Entities.pdf)
  - Act 44-2024 lets the Commissioner deny a licence for insufficient financial responsibility, experience or character, or for certain felony convictions. — [McConnell Valdés](https://www.mcvpr.com/newsroom-publications-IBE-IFE-Reform)
- **Cayman bank (CIMA):**
  - personal questionnaires, **two character references, a financial reference and police clearance** for each director, shareholder and officer;
  - evidence of two effective directors with banking experience;
  - a corporate chart;
  - auditor details and consent;
  - a business plan including customer base and management structure, with a **two-year projection**;
  - a recommendation letter from a bank or trust company;
  - parent supervisor approval where relevant;
  - memorandum and articles, certificate of incorporation and an opening balance sheet;
  - branches also give two international bank references and two years of annual reports.
  - Source: [CIMA](https://cima.ky/banking-services-regulatory-requirements)
- **Bahamas DARE:** principals must be fit and proper; exchanges must submit clearing and settlement rules; an AML compliance officer and an MLRO must be appointed. — [Chambers 2026](https://chambers.com/practice-guides/guides/fintech-2026/bahamas/law-and-practice)
- **Mauritius VAITOS:** an FSC-approved, Mauritius-resident senior executive and a physical office. — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-crypto-regulation/mauritius)

### Inferences
- A combined template would cover all of the above. The single most reusable artefact is the AML/CFT program and business risk assessment. Moneta's existing US-oriented AML work, much of which is Dakota's today, would need re-papering to each jurisdiction's statute (e.g., Bermuda's AML/ATF Regulations, Puerto Rico's BSA/OFAC independent audit).
- **Source-of-wealth evidence for every capital contributor** (Puerto Rico explicitly; Bermuda via funding-source disclosure) means investors in the licensed entity must be ready to share personal financials. This often surprises founders and seed investors.
- Bermuda's requirements for a pen test before go-live and smart-contract audits imply the product must be substantially built **before** licensing is complete, not after.

### Gaps
- Published document checklists for ADGM, VARA, the Bahamas (SCB application form), Gibraltar (GFSC DLT application pack), CNAD and Labuan were not retrieved in full.

## 3. FATF grey-list and EU high-risk status as of late 2026, and the effect on correspondent banking and card access

### Takeaway
After the **FATF October 2026 plenary (28–30 Oct 2026)** the only jurisdiction in this study on the FATF grey list is the **British Virgin Islands** (listed June 2025). The EU also added the BVI to its AML high-risk third-country list effective **29 Jan 2026**. None of the other study jurisdictions is FATF-listed:
- **Gibraltar** and the **UAE** left the FATF list in Feb 2024 and the EU list in Aug 2025.
- **Cayman** left the FATF list in Oct 2023 and the EU list in Feb 2024.
- **Mauritius** and the **Bahamas** left the EU list in 2022.
- **Bermuda**, **Puerto Rico (US)**, **El Salvador** and **Malaysia** are not listed.

Vanuatu sits on a different list, the EU **tax** blacklist. Listing triggers mandatory enhanced due diligence by EU and other banks. In practice that raises the cost and risk of correspondent relationships and partner onboarding.

### Inferences
- **Do not start a new licence in the BVI now.** It is FATF grey-listed and EU high-risk at the same time, so every EU-regulated bank, card BIN sponsor or stablecoin issuer would apply mandatory EDD. Tether's move out of the BVI is consistent with this.

  - Claims processing was still ongoing as of the trustee's Q2 2025 report. — [OCIF trustee report Q2 2025](https://docs.pr.gov/files/OCIF/AVISOS-PUBLICOS/Euro%20Pacific_Liquidation-Receiver-Trustee-Report-Q2-2025.pdf)

  - It entered a consent order in **October 2025**.
  - On **26 June 2026** OCIF appointed a compliance monitor, citing a liquidity shortfall that put obligations to depositors and **correspondent banking clients** at risk. — [OCIF monitor order](https://docs.pr.gov/files/OCIF/AVISOS-PUBLICOS/Banex%20International%20Bank,%20Inc.%20-%20Orden%20de%20Nombramiento%20de%20Monitor%20Cumplimiento_06.26.26.pdf)

### Inferences
- The strongest model for a consumer fiat + stablecoin bank offshore is **Xapo in Gibraltar**: a full bank licence, deposit insurance, USDC on and off rails into USD accounts, card-network membership, no US clients and a separate crypto-custody entity. It is also the most capital- and time-intensive model. Gibraltar's bank capital and timeline were not found.
- **Puerto Rico's IFE brand is damaged.** Two high-profile insolvency and AML liquidations (2022, 2026), Act 44-2024's sharp tightening (US$10M capital, local staff) and OCIF's active enforcement mean partners will diligence any new IFE intensely. The headline word "another" in Sept 2026 signals the pattern.
- Bermuda and the UAE are where large, US-connected crypto firms put **non-US** regulated entities (Coinbase in Bermuda), which suggests US infrastructure partners are comfortable with those regulators.

### Gaps
- A complete list of OCIF IFE revocations and closures for 2023–2026 and the current count of licensed IFEs; Puerto Rico IFEs' current crypto clients.
- Bahamas DARE stablecoin registrants and bank licensees active in crypto; Circle's licence status in Bermuda or elsewhere offshore (none confirmed).
- Xapo's current card-issuing arrangement (principal membership is sourced only to a company-supplied page).

## 5. How credible is each route with correspondent banks, card networks, stablecoin issuers and US infrastructure partners, and why are Anjouan/Mwali/Vanuatu-type licences red flags?

### Takeaway
Credibility tracks three things: list status (FATF/EU), regulator track record and the presence of real substance. ADGM, Bermuda, Gibraltar and Cayman (VASP) are the most bankable offshore regulators today. The Bahamas and Mauritius are mid-tier: clean lists but legacy reputational issues (FTX; historic listings). El Salvador and Dubai VARA are well known in crypto but less so with US and EU correspondents. Puerto Rico is US-law but carries a sector-specific stigma. The BVI is currently impaired by its listings. Anjouan, Mwali and similar island "licences" are treated as non-licences: the national central bank calls the issuers fictitious, and banks and processors refuse such entities.

### Cited Findings
- **Comoros island licences:**
  - The Central Bank of the Comoros calls AOFA and related Anjouan bodies "fictitious entities" (Dec 2025). — [Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/comoros-calls-anjouan-licensing-bodies-fictitious-seeks-uk-assistance/)
  - The World Bank and IMF flagged Comoros "offshore banking" schemes as illegal because only the Union government may regulate finance. — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/is-the-end-of-the-comoros-license-mirage-coming/)
  - SWIFT code checks for "Comoros" banks routinely fail, and payment systems flag them as high-risk. — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/is-the-end-of-the-comoros-license-mirage-coming/)
  - The ICC has warned that Anjouan banks are suspect. — [ICC](https://iccwbo.org/news-publications/news/anjouan-banks-suspect/)
- **Vanuatu:** EU tax-blacklisted for facilitating substance-less offshore structures; banks respond with extra checks and some cut ties. — [Council ST-5821-2026](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-5821-2026-INIT/en/pdf); [PMN News](https://pmn.co.nz/read/pacific-region/blacklisting-squeeze-hits-vanuatu-families-and-businesses-regulator-warns)
- **Correspondent access is the binding constraint:**
  - Correspondents limit USD clearing when they cannot verify crypto exposure. — [Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/blog/the-de-risking-paradox-why-cutting-off-digital-asset-businesses-increases-systemic-risk)
  - OCIF's Banex order explicitly cites risk to "correspondent banking clients." — [OCIF monitor order](https://docs.pr.gov/files/OCIF/AVISOS-PUBLICOS/Banex%20International%20Bank,%20Inc.%20-%20Orden%20de%20Nombramiento%20de%20Monitor%20Cumplimiento_06.26.26.pdf)
  - OCIF's draft regulation requires CEO and BSA-officer certification before an IFE offers correspondent services and lets OCIF demand the IFE's own correspondent contract. — [OCIF draft regulation](https://docs.pr.gov/files/OCIF/AVISOS-PUBLICOS/Proposed%20Regulation_International%20Banking%20and%20Financial%20Entities.pdf)
- **Stablecoin issuer integration precedents:**
  - Circle's USDC integrated with Gibraltar-licensed Xapo Bank. — [PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/xapo-bank-becomes-the-first-fully-licensed-bank-to-enable-usdc-deposits-and-withdrawals-301778907.html)
  - Coinbase's Bermuda exchange settles in USDC. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/crypto/2023/05/02/coinbase-to-launch-offshore-exchange-bermuda-this-week-after-regulatory-license-perpetual-futures/)
  - Tether is headquartered in El Salvador. — [Tether](https://tether.io/news/tether-licensed-in-el-salvador-strengthening-focus-on-emerging-markets-and-innovation/)
  - Wolfsberg's 2025 guidance gives banks a framework for banking stablecoin issuers. — [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/financial-services-law-advisor/2025/10/wolfsberg-guidance-crypto-crime-banking-risks)
- **US infrastructure:** a Puerto Rico IFE (FV Bank) publicly targeted a NY Fed master account. — [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2022/12/puerto-rico-chartered-crypto-bank-expects-to-open-fed-master-account-73067739). No confirmation that it was granted was found.

### Inferences
Credibility ranking. This is a synthesis of the findings above, not a sourced ranking.

| Route | List status (Nov 2026) | Partner credibility (inferred) | Main caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| ADGM FSRA (bank Cat 1 / VA / FRT) | Clean (FATF out Feb 2024; EU out Aug 2025) | High | US$10M for a bank; UAE substance and cost |

## 6. Realistic time and money to get licensed and open, per jurisdiction

### Takeaway
Hard published timelines are scarce. CIMA quotes 6–8 weeks for a *complete* bank application. OCIF gives 180 days to open after licensing. Bermuda and the others publish none. On money, published floors run from about US$100k (Bermuda Class M/F net assets; VARA custody about AED 600k ≈ US$163k) to US$2M (ADGM stablecoin issuer), US$10–11M (PR IFE, ADGM bank) and US$50M (El Salvador investment bank). On top of that come local office and staff, audit and legal costs, which were not found in primary sources.

### Cited Findings
- **Puerto Rico IFE:**
  - US$10M paid-in capital, US$1M unencumbered local assets, US$50k application fee, US$25k annual renewal and 8 PR FTEs. — [McConnell Valdés](https://www.mcvpr.com/newsroom-publications-IBE-IFE-Reform)
  - Operations must commence within 180 days of licensing. Incomplete applications are returned after 30 days. — [OCIF draft regulation](https://docs.pr.gov/files/OCIF/AVISOS-PUBLICOS/Proposed%20Regulation_International%20Banking%20and%20Financial%20Entities.pdf)
- **Cayman bank:** CI$400k (about US$480k) capital for an unrestricted licence. About 6–8 weeks for a complete application. Application, licence and annual fees are set by CIMA's fee schedule (amounts not retrieved). — [CIMA](https://cima.ky/banking-services-regulatory-requirements); [Conyers](https://www.conyers.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Banks_and_Trust_Companies_Licensing_and_Regulation-CAY.pdf)
- **Bermuda DABA:** application fees US$1,000 (T) and US$2,266 (M). — [BMA 2024 bulletin](https://www.bma.bm/viewPDF/documents/2024-02-21-11-17-19-Information-Bulletin-DAB-Application-ProcessFebruary-2024.pdf). Net assets of at least US$100k for M/F. — [Coincub](https://coincub.com/blog/bermuda-daba-licensing/) (secondary)
- **ADGM:** bank base capital US$10M. — [FSRA guide](https://assets.adgm.com/download/assets/Getting%2BStarted%2BGuide%2Bwith%2BFSRA.pdf/a8c5b07a589a11efa1111ed8b674bcd6). FRT issuer US$2M. — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/are/legal-updates/UAE-FSRA-consults-on-proposed-stablecoin-regulatory-framework-for-ADGM)
- **VARA:** custody AED 600k; transfer and settlement AED 500k; exchange AED 800k–1.5M, each stacked per activity. — [VARA Rulebook Part VI](https://rulebooks.vara.ae/entiresection/110)
- **Gibraltar DLT:** annual fee up to £90k cap plus £27,362. — [GFSC](https://www.fsc.gi/uploads/GFSC%20Fees%20Newsletter%202025_2026.pdf). Application fee about £45k. — [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/news/crypto-license-in-gibraltar-gfsc-dlt-provider-licence-2026-2027-guide-1538756) (unverified)
- **El Salvador investment bank:** US$50M. — [García Bodán](https://garciabodan.com/en/el-salvador-positions-itself-as-regional-financial-hub-new-investment-banking-law/). DASP pre-registration about 20 business days. — [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/news/bitcoin-service-provider-licence-el-salvador) (unverified)
- **Bahamas DARE:** capital proportional; exchanges must hold 12 months' opex in the Bahamas. — [Chambers 2026](https://chambers.com/practice-guides/guides/fintech-2026/bahamas/law-and-practice)

### Inferences
Rough planning estimates. These are **not sourced**: they are judgement calls from the dossier depth and capital figures above, to be validated by local counsel.

| Route | Regulatory capital (sourced where noted) | Other first-year cost drivers (inferred) | Time to licence and open (inferred) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Puerto Rico IFE | US$10M + US$1M local assets (sourced) | 8+ PR staff, independent director, BSA audit, US$50k fee | 12–24 months |
| ADGM bank (Cat 1) | US$10M base (sourced) | UAE office and senior staff, Category 1 prudential reporting | 18–30 months |

| Dubai VARA (custody + transfer) | ≈ AED 1.1M combined (sourced per activity) | Dubai office, staff | 9–18 months |
| Bermuda DABA Class M → F | ≥ US$100k net assets (secondary) plus a capital model | Bermuda head-office staff, pen tests, insurance | 6–12 months for M; F later |
| Bermuda bank | not found | Very high | 18–36 months |
| Cayman VASP licence | not found | Cayman office or registered agent | 6–12 months |
| Bahamas DARE | proportional; 12 months' opex locally for exchanges (sourced) | Bahamas presence | 6–12 months |
| Gibraltar DLT | proportional (secondary) | ~£45k fee (unverified), resident management | 9–18 months |
| Gibraltar bank (Xapo path) | not found | Very high | 2+ years |
| El Salvador DASP | low (unverified) | Local presence | 1–6 months |
| El Salvador investment bank | US$50M (sourced) | High | 12+ months; not a retail route |
| Mauritius VAITOS Class R | not found | Resident senior executive and office | 6–12 months |
| Anjouan / Mwali / Vanuatu "bank" | nominal | Unusable with partners | n/a: do not pursue |

- For a pre-launch team, the cheapest **credible** offshore step is probably a Bermuda Class M (or Class T to pilot) or a Cayman/Bahamas VASP-type licence, with fiat handled by a partner bank. An ADGM licence costs more but carries the most institutional credibility, which fits the "institutional multi-jurisdiction treasury" pivot. A full offshore *bank* only makes sense once there is roughly US$15–25M of committed capital (regulatory capital plus operating runway). That threshold is an inference.
- Counsel must decide: the choice of entity, jurisdiction and licence class; whether the product's fiat leg (ACH deposits, bank withdrawals) can be run through a partner such as Dakota or another bank under an offshore licence; tax and substance (CFC, economic-substance laws); and US-person restrictions (covered by another researcher).

### Gaps
- No regulator published a binding end-to-end timeline except CIMA (6–8 weeks, complete application only) and OCIF's 180-day post-licence opening window.
- Legal, audit, office and staffing cost benchmarks per jurisdiction were not found in reliable sources.
- No first-hand time-to-licence data was found for recent Bermuda, ADGM, VARA, Cayman or Bahamas licensees.
